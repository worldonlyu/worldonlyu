# Reading and Running a Brain: Neural Recording, Large-Scale Simulation, Neuromorphic Hardware

> An emulation needs a way to read a brain's state, a machine to run the copy on, and a model of what to compute at each node; this chapter covers all three. Electrode recording has doubled its simultaneous neuron count every six to seven years since the 1950s and now reaches a few thousand units per experiment, imaging covers most of a larval zebrafish brain, and connectomics has delivered complete insect wiring. Billion-neuron point-neuron networks have run since 2009, yet the only whole-brain model that predicts behaviour is the fly, and no reconstruction project has produced a mammalian model that learns or behaves. Neuromorphic chips are built for energy, not fidelity, and every data-constrained brain model still runs on CPUs or GPUs. Compute is affordable at the fidelity anyone can parameterize; the binding constraint is the measurements to load into it.

## Why this matters for the project

Chapters 06 and 07 covered what a connectome gives and what preservation keeps. This chapter asks what it would take to run a scanned or recorded brain, and how far the equipment is from cat or human scale.

Three numbers frame the answer. The best laboratory probes and human implants read on the order of 10^3 channels at once [Steinmetz et al., 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf4588). A cat cerebral cortex holds about 2.5 × 10^8 neurons by direct count [Jardim-Messeder et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2017.00118), against an older figure near 10^9 discussed below; a human brain has about 8.6 × 10^10. And simultaneously recorded neuron counts have doubled only every six to seven years [Stevenson and Kording, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731). Recording alone will not read a mammalian brain on any timescale that matters to a person alive today.

On the running side the picture inverts. A cat-scale network of simple spiking neurons ran on a 2009 supercomputer [Ananthanarayanan et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1654059.1654124), and a crude spiking model wired exactly by the fly connectome predicts real behaviour on a laptop [Shiu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9). At the fidelity anyone can parameterize, compute is not the obstacle; nobody can measure the synaptic weights, modulatory state and plasticity rules a higher-fidelity model would need, or knows which fidelity is needed. For this project: cat-scale spike routing has been demonstrated at point-neuron fidelity, far below real time; the knowledge of what to load into such a model does not exist, and the gap is scientific, not industrial.

## Key ideas

**Recording doubling time.** Stevenson and Kording showed in 2011 that simultaneously recorded neuron counts had grown exponentially since the 1950s, doubling about every seven years (the fitted value over 56 studies is usually quoted as 7.4 ± 0.4 years) [Stevenson and Kording, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731); a 2026 retrospective revisits the fit [Musacchio, 2026](https://www.fabriziomusacchio.com/blog/2026-02-05-moores_law_for_neural_recordings/). Stevenson's maintained dataset, with points to about 2020, fits 6.3 ± 0.2 years over 92 studies [Stevenson, 2015](https://github.com/ihstevenson/scaling), a bootstrapped fit computed by the repository's script and printed in its plot title, not a peer-reviewed result, so "about 6 to 7 years" is the safe statement. It describes electrodes; imaging has jumped ahead.

**Channels versus neurons.** An electrode count is not a count of neurons read; stable, well-isolated single units are typically fewer than channels. Against 8.6 × 10^10 human neurons, or a few times 10^8 for a cat, that ratio is what matters.

**Recording modalities.** Neuropixels probes are CMOS shanks with thousands of recording sites; Neuropixels 2.0 has more than 5,000 sites per probe, multiple shanks and a smaller package, and tracked the same neurons for more than two months [Steinmetz et al., 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf4588). Light-sheet calcium imaging captures most of the roughly 10^5 neurons of a transparent larval zebrafish at about one volume per second, glia included [Mu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050), but gives activity, not connectivity or synaptic weights.

**Neuron models.** A leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) point neuron is one membrane voltage that integrates input with leak and spikes at threshold: the neuron of the fly whole-brain model, IBM's cat-scale run and most neuromorphic chips, blind to dendrites and ion channels. A multi-compartment neuron has hundreds of coupled compartments with Hodgkin-Huxley-type channels fitted to morphology and electrophysiology, as in the Blue Brain microcircuit [Markram et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.09.029), at 10^3 to 10^4 times the compute. Generalized LIF (GLIF) point neurons are fitted to recorded cells so that a point network reproduces a biophysical one; the Allen V1 model ships in both forms [Billeh et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.040).

**Emulation level, mechanistic versus functional.** Sandberg and Bostrom's ladder runs from analog population models through spiking networks and compartmental electrophysiology to metabolome, proteome, protein-complex states and single-molecule stochastics, each with a compute estimate for simulating the physics at that level [Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008](https://www.futureofhumanityinstitute.org/s/2008-3.pdf). Functional estimates such as Carlsmith's ask instead how much compute would match the brain's task performance [Carlsmith, 2020](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/how-much-computational-power-does-it-take-to-match-the-human-brain/). Conflating the two is the commonest error in uploading arithmetic, and which rung is needed is unknown.

**Data bottleneck.** For low-fidelity emulation the limiting resource is not operations per second but the measured wiring, synaptic strengths, neuromodulatory state and plasticity rules to load, none yet obtainable at mammalian scale.

## State of the field (as of 2026-10-09)

Sources were checked by web search on 2026-10-09, and package versions against PyPI and GitHub; the few figures that could not be confirmed are marked where they occur.

**Reading.** Electrode recording sits at a few thousand simultaneous units per experiment on a curve doubling every six to seven years [Stevenson and Kording, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731); [Stevenson, 2015](https://github.com/ihstevenson/scaling). Neuropixels 2.0 [Steinmetz et al., 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf4588) is the workhorse. The International Brain Laboratory pooled standardized Neuropixels recordings from 12 laboratories into a brain-wide map of mouse activity: 621,733 neurons from 699 probes in 139 mice across 279 brain areas, of which about 75,708 passed quality control by IBL's account, and 31,344 fill the analysis table shipped with its code [International Brain Laboratory et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09235-0); the fall from the first number to the last is the channels-versus-neurons problem in one dataset. In larval zebrafish, whole-brain light-sheet recording of neurons and glia showed radial astrocytes accumulating evidence that swimming is futile and then suppressing it [Mu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050), a computation a neurons-only emulation would miss.

Human implants are an order of magnitude below that. Neuralink's N1 records through 1,024 electrodes on 64 threads [Neuralink, 2026](https://www.neuralink.com/); the first human implant was in January 2024, 12 people had received implants by September 2025 [Seeking Alpha, 2025](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4493634-elon-musks-neuralink-says-globally-12-people-have-received-implants) and 21 participants were enrolled by January 2026 [Yahoo News, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/elon-musks-neuralink-says-21-183038116.html); its visual prosthesis Blindsight received FDA Breakthrough Device designation in September 2024 [MobiHealthNews, 2024](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/elon-musk-s-neuralink-device-blindsight-gets-fda-breakthrough-device-designation). Precision Neuroscience's Layer 7 Cortical Interface, a 1,024-electrode surface film, received FDA 510(k) clearance on 17 April 2025 for implantation of up to 30 days [Precision Neuroscience, 2025](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/precision-neuroscience-receives-fda-clearance-for-high-resolution-cortical-electrode-array). Paradromics' Connexus (421 electrodes per module) was implanted for about ten minutes in a consenting epilepsy patient at the University of Michigan on 14 May 2025, announced 2 June 2025 [Yahoo News, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/paradromics-implants-brain-computer-interface-221659162.html), and received its first long-term implant in the Connect-One feasibility study in June 2026 [Michigan Medicine, 2026](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/news-release/university-michigan-implants-first-human-paradromics-wireless-brain-computer-interface-designed). Synchron's endovascular Stentrode met its safety endpoint in all six participants of the COMMAND feasibility study [Synchron, 2024](https://www.biospace.com/news/synchron-announces-positive-results-from-u-s-command-study-of-endovascular-brain-computer-interface). All of these are company announcements, and peer-reviewed reports lag. At a 6.3-year doubling from 10^3 units, 10^9 neurons is about 20 doublings (roughly 125 years) and 10^11 about 27 (roughly 170 years): an illustration, not a forecast, saying only that electrodes will not do it alone.

Connectomics supplies the wiring. FlyWire delivered a complete adult fly brain of 139,255 neurons and about 5 × 10^7 synapses [Dorkenwald et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y). The Janelia MaleCNS connectome of the complete male fly central nervous system, 166,691 neurons across brain and nerve cord, was released as a preprint in October 2025 and published in Cell in September 2026 [Berg et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015); FlyWire annotation release 3.0.0 was cross-validated against it. The mammalian frontier is a cubic millimetre of mouse cortex (chapter 06), and synaptic strengths, neuromodulator state and plasticity rules remain unmeasured at scale in every species.

**Running.** The landmark simulations:

| Model | Neurons | Synapses | Neuron model | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Izhikevich and Edelman, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0712231105) | 10^6 | ~5 × 10^8 | Multi-compartment; human DTI-derived anatomy | Brain-like rhythms; no function |
| IBM cat-scale [Ananthanarayanan et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1654059.1654124) | 1.6 × 10^9 | 8.9 × 10^12 | Phenomenological point neurons | Spike routing at cat scale, far slower than real time |
| Spaun [Eliasmith et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225266) | 2.5 × 10^6 | n/a | Spiking neurons in a designed cognitive architecture | Eight tasks; not anatomically reconstructed |
| Blue Brain microcircuit [Markram et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.09.029) | ~31,000 | ~3.7 × 10^7 | Multi-compartment; 207 morpho-electrical types | In vivo-like regimes in 0.29 mm^3 of juvenile rat somatosensory cortex; no learning or behaviour |
| Allen mouse V1 [Billeh et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.040) | ~2.3 × 10^5 | n/a | Biophysical core plus matched GLIF version, constrained by Neuropixels and imaging data | Validated against recordings; downloadable |
| Fly whole brain [Shiu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9) | ~1.3 × 10^5 | FlyWire connectome | LIF; weights proportional to synapse counts; no fitted dynamics | Predicted feeding circuits; validated optogenetically |

IBM's run used the Dawn Blue Gene/P (147,456 cores, 144 TB) and received the 2009 ACM Gordon Bell Prize. Henry Markram, in an open letter to IBM's chief technology officer copied to the press, called it a hoax, because point neurons without measured biology are not a cat brain; the paper itself had claimed only that its simulations exceed the scale of the cat cortex [IEEE Spectrum, 2009](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/blue-brain-project-leader-angry-about-cat-brain-2650252456). Both were right: the run proved that spike traffic at cat scale fits in a supercomputer, and nothing about cats. The fly model is the clearest success: LIF neurons wired exactly by the FlyWire connectome predicted which neurons drive feeding when sugar sensors are activated, the prediction held optogenetically, and it runs in Brian2 on a laptop.

The Blue Brain Project ran from 2005 to the end of 2024 as a Swiss national research infrastructure [EPFL Blue Brain Project, 2024](https://bbp.epfl.ch/bbp/research/domains/bluebrain/index.html%3Fp=12290.html). Its stack (Neurodamus, BlueCelluLab, BluePyOpt, Blue Brain SNAP) moved to the Open Brain Institute, a non-profit launched in 2025; BlueBrain/neurodamus was archived on 26 February 2025 [Blue Brain Project and Open Brain Institute, 2025](https://github.com/BlueBrain/neurodamus), and OBI's OBI-ONE workflow library is on PyPI (release 2026.10.8, 9 October 2026). The Human Brain Project ran 2013 to 2023 on 607 million euros [Human Brain Project, 2023](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/human-brain-project-concludes-highlighting-key-achievements-in-integrative-brain-research/); EBRAINS continues under an EBRAINS 2.0 grant that began in January 2024 [EBRAINS, 2023](https://ebrains.eu/news-and-events/2023/seasons-greetings-from-ebrains). The Allen toolkit BMTK reached 1.2.0 on PyPI on 24 June 2026 with the DPointNet GPU backend (TensorFlow, CUDA ops, backpropagation-through-time learning rules for GLIF networks); its documentation describes a 66,658-neuron V1 plus LGN model on V100 to H200 GPUs.

**Neuromorphic hardware.** Intel's Loihi 2 (about 10^6 neurons per chip) was scaled in April 2024 to Hala Point: 1,152 chips, 1.15 × 10^9 neurons, 1.28 × 10^11 synapses, about 2.6 kW (company figures, unverified). Intel archived its Lava framework on 13 May 2026 (last release v0.10.0, 8 August 2024) and says a next-generation Loihi and SDK are in development. SpiNNaker2 (TU Dresden and SpiNNcloud) ships 48-chip boards of 152-core ARM chips; its py-spinnaker2 interface is a public GitLab repository in a v0.8.x series (v0.8.0 5 August 2026; v0.8.2 23 September 2026), but the chip-side code it depends on, the s2-sim2lab-app project, is private and access must be requested from SpiNNcloud or Bernhard Vogginger. BrainScaleS-2 (Heidelberg) is analog and about a thousand times faster than biology. The Neuromorphic Intermediate Representation [Pedersen et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52259-9) gives these a common format; as of 2026 the project lists nine simulators and five hardware platforms, including Loihi 2 via Lava-DL, SpiNNaker2 and BrainScaleS-2, more than the 2024 paper evaluated. All target energy per synaptic event, not fidelity; none has run a data-constrained brain model larger than an insect, and the V1, Blue Brain and fly models all run on CPUs or GPUs.

**Compute.** Sandberg and Bostrom's roadmap tabulates human emulation by level [Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008](https://www.futureofhumanityinstitute.org/s/2008-3.pdf); its figures below, Carlsmith's, and the 2026 machine figures are from memory, unchecked against their sources this session.

| Level | Human (FLOPS) | Cat, scaled by about 10^-2 | Status in 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spiking network | ~10^18, ~10^4 TB | ~10^16 | One supercomputer; IBM did the cat case in 2009 |
| Compartmental electrophysiology | ~10^22 | ~10^20 | Cat: edge of one large AI cluster. Human: about 10^4 beyond one machine |
| Metabolome, proteome, protein complexes | 10^25 to 10^27 | 10^23 to 10^25 | Out of reach |
| Single-molecule stochastics | ~10^43 | ~10^41 | Far out of reach |

Table note. Cat column: this chapter's own scaling of the human figures by neuron count, not from the source. Human column: from memory of the roadmap's table, unchecked against the report this session (see reference 14).

Carlsmith's functional estimate puts 10^13 to 10^17 FLOP/s as the likely range to match the brain's task performance, 10^15 more likely than not sufficient, under 10 percent on needing more than 10^21 [Carlsmith, 2020](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/how-much-computational-power-does-it-take-to-match-the-human-brain/). The largest 2026 supercomputers deliver about 2 × 10^18 FP64 FLOP/s, the largest AI clusters 10^20 to 10^21 in low precision. Brain simulation is memory- and communication-bound rather than FLOP-bound, so every row is a lower bound, and in every row the constraint is the same: nobody has the parameters to put into the FLOPs.

## Debates and critiques

**Scale versus biology.** The IBM and Markram exchange set the template for every scale claim since: a billion point neurons is a routing benchmark, not a brain, and a benchmark is still worth having [Ananthanarayanan et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1654059.1654124). Hala Point's 1.15 × 10^9 neurons should be read the same way: neuromorphic systems run hand-designed or trained networks and lack the memory per neuron for measured biophysics, and Intel archiving Lava in May 2026 shows how volatile the platforms are, which NIR [Pedersen et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52259-9) tries to answer. The cat cortex figure of about 7.6 × 10^8 that IBM used is also roughly three times isotropic-fractionator estimates of about 2.5 × 10^8 (chapter 05), shifting every cat-scale estimate here by the same factor. Which count the 7.6 × 10^8 is should be stated: IBM presented it as a cortical figure and this chapter and chapter 06 use it that way, while the README and chapter 08 treat a figure of that size as the whole cat brain; its provenance was not checked this session, and the only direct count cited in this notebook is the cortical 2.5 × 10^8.

**Reconstruction versus function.** Critics of Blue Brain and the Human Brain Project argue that on the order of a billion euros bought no model that learns or behaves; defenders point to tools (NEURON and CoreNEURON, SONATA, EBRAINS), atlases and the microcircuit itself [Markram et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.09.029). Spaun performs tasks with far fewer neurons [Eliasmith et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225266) but copies no particular brain, which is what this project cares about. The value-for-money question is unresolved.

**Does structure plus simple dynamics suffice?** The fly model's validated predictions support "structure carries function" for insect sensorimotor circuits [Shiu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9). Sceptics note that it used synapse counts as weights, ignored neuromodulators, gap junctions, glia and plasticity, and succeeded on feedforward-dominated pathways; the zebrafish glia result [Mu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050) shows what it leaves out. Transfer to recurrent mammalian cortex is open.

**What counts as reading a brain?** Recording advocates count units; emulation sceptics reply that activity without synaptic weights, molecular state and plasticity rules cannot parameterize a model, and that connectomics gives structure but is destructive and static. BCI milestones come mainly from company communications, peer-reviewed reports lag, and channel counts of about 10^3 are routinely conflated with neurons read.

**Thirty orders of magnitude.** Mechanistic levels (10^15 to 10^43 FLOPS for a human) and functional estimates (10^13 to 10^17 FLOP/s) are routinely compared as if they measured the same thing [Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008](https://www.futureofhumanityinstitute.org/s/2008-3.pdf); [Carlsmith, 2020](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/how-much-computational-power-does-it-take-to-match-the-human-brain/). Copying a particular brain needs the first; matching brain-level capability needs the second.

## Open questions

1. What is the minimal fidelity at which an emulation preserves behaviour, memory and, if anything, experience: point neurons, compartments, synaptic molecular state, or lower? No experiment yet discriminates these for any mammal.
2. Can synaptic weights, short-term dynamics and plasticity rules be measured at scale from any modality, or must they be inferred from activity, and with what identifiability? Do glia, neuromodulators and gap junctions raise the required level further?
3. Can the fly result (about 10^5 neurons) be reproduced for a mouse (about 7 × 10^7) once a whole-brain mouse connectome exists, and what fails first, dynamics or data?
4. Is there any non-destructive route to a mammalian connectome, or is emulation necessarily a destructive scan followed by reconstruction?
5. Will electrode recording keep its 6.3-year doubling, saturate on tissue damage, heat and bandwidth, or be superseded by optical and molecular readout, and what is the ceiling for chronic human implants?
6. How should an emulation be validated (population statistics, single-trial behaviour, or the specific animal's memories), and what does compartmental fidelity (10^20 to 10^22 FLOPS) really cost once memory and communication limits are counted?

## Where to start

1. **Run the fly brain in a browser (free, one afternoon).** The connectome-constrained Drosophila LIF model (Brian2; FlyWire v630, also v783) has a Colab notebook for activating or silencing neurons by FlyWire ID and watching the predicted spiking: https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model. Read the code to see which assumptions it makes.
2. **Read the two free compute reports.** Carlsmith (2020) for functional estimates, then the Sandberg and Bostrom roadmap for the fidelity table (the FHI closed in 2024; a mirror may be needed). Eliasmith's How to Build a Brain (Oxford University Press, 2013; paid) is the functional-modelling counterpoint. None of the three could be opened this session.
3. **Watch the UCL Neuropixels Course (free).** Run since May 2017, with YouTube playlists from the 2020 edition through 2025, linked with manuals and probe papers at https://github.com/Julie-Fabre/awesome_neuropixels.
4. **Install two simulators (free).** `pip install nengo` (4.1.0, May 2025; the framework behind Spaun; https://www.nengo.ai/nengo) and `pip install nest-simulator` (3.10.0, June 2026; the standard large-scale point-neuron simulator; https://nest-simulator.org). Build a 10^4-neuron balanced network, time it, and work out how far you are from 10^9.
5. **Run a mammalian model and look at real data (free, workstation).** BMTK tutorials (https://alleninstitute.github.io/bmtk/, `pip install bmtk`) and the mouse V1 models at https://portal.brain-map.org/explore/models/mv1-all-layers; the GLIF version runs on a workstation, the biophysical version needs a cluster. Then the IBL brain-wide map through the ONE API (https://github.com/int-brain-lab/paper-brain-wide-map) and the FlyWire connectome through fafbseg-py (`pip install fafbseg`, 3.2.2, February 2026) and Codex (https://codex.flywire.ai).
