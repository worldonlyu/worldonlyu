# Reading and Running a Brain: Neural Recording, Large-Scale Simulation, Neuromorphic Hardware

> An emulation needs a way to read a brain's state, a machine to run the copy on, and a model of what to compute at each node; this chapter covers all three. Electrode recording has doubled its simultaneous neuron count every six to seven years since the 1950s and now reaches a few thousand units per experiment, imaging covers most of a larval zebrafish brain, and connectomics has delivered complete insect wiring. Billion-neuron point-neuron networks have run since 2009, yet the only whole-brain model that predicts behaviour is the fly, and no reconstruction project has produced a mammalian model that learns or behaves. Neuromorphic chips are built for energy, not fidelity, and every data-constrained brain model still runs on CPUs or GPUs. Compute is affordable at the fidelity anyone can parameterize; the binding constraint is the measurements to load into it.

## Why this matters for the project

Chapters 06 and 07 covered what a connectome gives and what preservation keeps. This chapter asks what it would take to run a scanned or recorded brain, and how far the equipment is from cat or human scale.

Three numbers frame the answer. The best laboratory probes and human implants read on the order of 10^3 channels at once [Steinmetz et al., 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf4588). A cat cerebral cortex holds about 2.5 × 10^8 neurons by direct count [Jardim-Messeder et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2017.00118), against an older figure near 10^9 discussed below; a human brain has about 8.6 × 10^10. And simultaneously recorded neuron counts have doubled only every six to seven years [Stevenson and Kording, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731). Recording alone will not read a mammalian brain on any timescale that matters to a person alive today.

On the running side the picture inverts. A cat-scale network of simple point neurons ran on a 2009 supercomputer, far below real time [Ananthanarayanan et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1654059.1654124), and a crude spiking model wired exactly by the fly connectome predicts real behaviour on a laptop [Shiu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9). At the fidelity anyone can parameterize, compute is not the obstacle; what nobody can do is measure the synaptic weights, modulatory state and plasticity rules a higher-fidelity model would need, or say which fidelity is needed. For this project the gap is scientific, not industrial.

## Key ideas

**Recording doubling time.** Stevenson and Kording showed in 2011 that simultaneously recorded neuron counts had grown exponentially since the 1950s, doubling about every seven years (fitted value about 7.4 years) [Stevenson and Kording, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731); a 2026 retrospective revisits the fit [Musacchio, 2026](https://www.fabriziomusacchio.com/blog/2026-02-05-moores_law_for_neural_recordings/). Stevenson's maintained dataset, to about 2020, fits 6.3 ± 0.2 years over 92 studies [Stevenson, 2015](https://github.com/ihstevenson/scaling), a bootstrapped fit printed in the plot title, not a peer-reviewed result; "about 6 to 7 years" is the safe statement, and it describes electrodes, not imaging.

**Channels versus neurons.** An electrode count is not a count of neurons read; stable single units are typically fewer than channels, and against 8.6 × 10^10 human neurons, or a few times 10^8 for a cat, that ratio is what matters.

**Recording modalities.** Neuropixels probes are CMOS shanks with thousands of sites; Neuropixels 2.0 has more than 5,000 per probe on multiple shanks and tracked the same neurons for over two months [Steinmetz et al., 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf4588). Light-sheet calcium imaging captures most of the roughly 10^5 neurons of a transparent larval zebrafish at about one volume per second, glia included [Mu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050), but gives activity, not connectivity or synaptic weights.

**Neuron models.** A leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) point neuron is one membrane voltage that integrates input with leak and spikes at threshold: the neuron of the fly whole-brain model, IBM's cat-scale run and most neuromorphic chips, blind to dendrites and ion channels. A multi-compartment neuron has hundreds of coupled compartments with Hodgkin-Huxley-type channels fitted to morphology and electrophysiology, as in the Blue Brain microcircuit [Markram et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.09.029), at 10^3 to 10^4 times the compute. Generalized LIF (GLIF) point neurons are fitted to recorded cells so that a point network reproduces a biophysical one; the Allen V1 model ships in both forms [Billeh et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.01.040).

**Emulation level, mechanistic versus functional.** Sandberg and Bostrom's ladder runs from analog population models through spiking networks and compartmental electrophysiology to metabolome, proteome, protein-complex states and single-molecule stochastics, each with a compute estimate [Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008](https://www.futureofhumanityinstitute.org/s/2008-3.pdf). Functional estimates such as Carlsmith's ask instead how much compute would match the brain's task performance [Carlsmith, 2020](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/how-much-computational-power-does-it-take-to-match-the-human-brain/). Conflating the two is the commonest error in uploading arithmetic, and which rung is needed is unknown.

## State of the field (as of 2026-10-09)

**Reading.** Electrode recording sits at a few thousand simultaneous units per experiment, with Neuropixels 2.0 [Steinmetz et al., 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf4588) the workhorse. The International Brain Laboratory pooled standardized Neuropixels recordings from 12 laboratories into a brain-wide map of mouse activity: 621,733 neurons from 699 probes in 139 mice across 279 brain areas, of which 31,344 fill the analysis table shipped with its code [International Brain Laboratory et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09235-0). In larval zebrafish, whole-brain light-sheet recording of neurons and glia showed radial astrocytes accumulating evidence that swimming is futile and then suppressing it [Mu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050), a computation a neurons-only emulation would miss.

Human implants are an order of magnitude below that. Neuralink's N1 records through 1,024 electrodes on 64 threads [Neuralink, 2026](https://www.neuralink.com/); the first human implant was in January 2024, 12 people had implants by September 2025 [Seeking Alpha, 2025](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4493634-elon-musks-neuralink-says-globally-12-people-have-received-implants) and 21 participants were enrolled by January 2026 [Yahoo News, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/elon-musks-neuralink-says-21-183038116.html); its visual prosthesis Blindsight has held FDA Breakthrough Device designation since September 2024 [MobiHealthNews, 2024](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/elon-musk-s-neuralink-device-blindsight-gets-fda-breakthrough-device-designation). Precision Neuroscience's 1,024-electrode Layer 7 Cortical Interface received FDA 510(k) clearance on 17 April 2025 for implants of up to 30 days [Precision Neuroscience, 2025](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/precision-neuroscience-receives-fda-clearance-for-high-resolution-cortical-electrode-array). Paradromics' Connexus (421 electrodes per module) was implanted for about ten minutes in a consenting epilepsy patient on 14 May 2025, announced 2 June [Yahoo News, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/paradromics-implants-brain-computer-interface-221659162.html), with a first long-term implant in June 2026 [Michigan Medicine, 2026](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/news-release/university-michigan-implants-first-human-paradromics-wireless-brain-computer-interface-designed). Synchron's endovascular Stentrode met its safety endpoint in all six COMMAND participants [Synchron, 2024](https://www.biospace.com/news/synchron-announces-positive-results-from-u-s-command-study-of-endovascular-brain-computer-interface). At a 6.3-year doubling from 10^3 units, 10^9 neurons is about 20 doublings (roughly 125 years) and 10^11 about 27 (roughly 170 years): an illustration, not a forecast, saying only that electrodes will not do it alone.

Connectomics supplies the wiring. FlyWire delivered a complete adult fly brain of 139,255 neurons and about 5 × 10^7 synapses [Dorkenwald et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07558-y). Janelia's MaleCNS connectome of the complete male fly central nervous system (166,691 neurons, brain and nerve cord) followed in 2026 [Berg et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015); FlyWire annotation release 3.0.0 was cross-validated against it. The mammalian frontier is a cubic millimetre of mouse cortex (chapter 06), and synaptic strengths, neuromodulator state and plasticity rules remain unmeasured at scale in every species.

**Running.** The landmark simulations:

| Model | Neurons | Synapses | Neuron model | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| IBM cat-scale [Ananthanarayanan et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1654059.1654124) | 1.6 × 10^9 | 8.9 × 10^12 | Phenomenological point neurons | Spike routing at cat scale, far slower than real time |
| Spaun [Eliasmith et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225266) | 2.5 × 10^6 | n/a | Spiking neurons in a designed cognitive architecture | Eight tasks; not anatomically reconstructed |
| Blue Brain microcircuit [Markram et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.09.029) | ~31,000 | ~3.7 × 10^7 | Multi-compartment; 207 morpho-electrical types | In vivo-like regimes in 0.29 mm^3 of juvenile rat somatosensory cortex; no learning or behaviour |

| Fly whole brain [Shiu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9) | ~1.3 × 10^5 | FlyWire connectome | LIF; weights proportional to synapse counts; no fitted dynamics | Predicted feeding circuits; validated optogenetically |

IBM's run used the Dawn Blue Gene/P (147,456 cores, 144 TB) and received the 2009 ACM Gordon Bell Prize. Henry Markram called it a hoax in an open letter to IBM copied to the press, because point neurons without measured biology are not a cat brain; the paper itself had claimed only that its simulations exceed the scale of the cat cortex [IEEE Spectrum, 2009](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/blue-brain-project-leader-angry-about-cat-brain-2650252456). Both were right: the run proved that spike traffic at cat scale fits in a supercomputer, and nothing about cats. The fly model is the clearest success: LIF neurons wired by the FlyWire connectome predicted which neurons drive feeding from sugar input, the prediction held optogenetically, and it runs in Brian2 on a laptop.

The Blue Brain Project ran from 2005 to the end of 2024 as a Swiss national research infrastructure [EPFL Blue Brain Project, 2024](https://bbp.epfl.ch/bbp/research/domains/bluebrain/index.html%3Fp=12290.html). Its software stack moved to the Open Brain Institute, a non-profit launched in 2025 (BlueBrain/neurodamus archived 26 February 2025 [Blue Brain Project and Open Brain Institute, 2025](https://github.com/BlueBrain/neurodamus); OBI-ONE 2026.10.8 on PyPI, 9 October 2026). The Human Brain Project ran 2013 to 2023 on 607 million euros [Human Brain Project, 2023](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/human-brain-project-concludes-highlighting-key-achievements-in-integrative-brain-research/); EBRAINS continues under an EBRAINS 2.0 grant that began in January 2024 [EBRAINS, 2023](https://ebrains.eu/news-and-events/2023/seasons-greetings-from-ebrains).
**Neuromorphic hardware.** Intel's Loihi 2 was scaled in April 2024 to Hala Point at Sandia National Laboratories: 1,152 chips, 1.15 × 10^9 neurons, 1.28 × 10^11 synapses, at most 2,600 W [Intel, 2024](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system). Intel archived its Lava framework on 13 May 2026 (last release v0.10.0, August 2024) and says a next-generation Loihi and SDK are in development [Intel, 2026](https://github.com/lava-nc/lava). SpiNNaker2 (TU Dresden and SpiNNcloud) is a 152-core ARM chip holding about 150,000 spiking neurons, shipped on 48-chip boards of 7,296 cores [heise online, 2024](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Neuromorphic-supercomputer-SpiNNaker-2-can-be-rented-in-the-cloud-9712597.html); its py-spinnaker2 interface is public on GitLab (v0.8.2, 23 September 2026), the chip-side s2-sim2lab-app code private. BrainScaleS-2 (Heidelberg) pairs an analog accelerator core with digital processors [Pehle et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.795876) and runs about a thousand times faster than biology [Müller et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2022.884128). Western Sydney University's DeepSouth, an FPGA system announced in December 2023 for 228 trillion synaptic operations per second, has no operational report that search could find [Western Sydney University, 2023](https://www.westernsydney.edu.au/newscentre/news_centre/more_news_stories/world_first_supercomputer_capable_of_brain-scale_simulation_being_built_at_western_sydney_university). The Neuromorphic Intermediate Representation [Pedersen et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52259-9) gives these a common format; its repository now lists 9 simulators and 5 hardware platforms, against 7 and 4 in the paper. All target energy per synaptic event, not fidelity; none has run a data-constrained brain model larger than an insect, and the V1, Blue Brain and fly models all run on CPUs or GPUs.

**Compute.** Sandberg and Bostrom's roadmap tabulates human emulation by level [Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008](https://www.futureofhumanityinstitute.org/s/2008-3.pdf). The table below paraphrases it: the 10^18 (spiking) and 10^43 (single molecules) entries appear in search results, the intermediate figures are approximate, and the cat column is this chapter's derivation.

| Level | Human (FLOPS) | Cat, scaled by about 10^-2 | Status in 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spiking network | ~10^18, ~10^4 TB | ~10^16 | One supercomputer; IBM did the cat case in 2009 |
| Compartmental electrophysiology | ~10^22 | ~10^20 | Cat: edge of one large AI cluster. Human: about 10^4 beyond one machine |
| Metabolome, proteome, protein complexes | 10^25 to 10^27 | 10^23 to 10^25 | Out of reach |
| Single-molecule stochastics | ~10^43 | ~10^41 | Far out of reach |

Table note. Cat column: human figures scaled by about 10^-2, the ratio of the IBM-era cat cortex figure of about 7.6 × 10^8 (the scale of the 2009 hardware claim) to 8.6 × 10^10 human neurons; with the direct cortical count of 2.5 × 10^8 the factor is about 3 × 10^-3 and every cat entry falls by a further factor of three.

Carlsmith's functional estimate concludes that about 10^15 FLOP/s is more likely than not enough to match the brain's task performance, with under 10 percent on needing more than 10^21 [Carlsmith, 2020](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/how-much-computational-power-does-it-take-to-match-the-human-brain/). The June 2026 TOP500 list is led by LineShine at 2.2 × 10^18 FP64 FLOP/s, with El Capitan at 1.8 × 10^18 [TOP500, 2026](https://top500.org/lists/top500/2026/06/highs); the largest AI clusters are estimated at 10^20 to 10^21 in low precision (not checked here). Brain simulation is memory- and communication-bound rather than FLOP-bound, so every row is a lower bound, and in every row the constraint is the same: nobody has the parameters to put into the FLOPs.

## Debates and critiques

**Scale versus biology.** The IBM and Markram exchange set the template for every scale claim since: a billion point neurons is a routing benchmark, not a brain, and a benchmark is still worth having [Ananthanarayanan et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1145/1654059.1654124); [IEEE Spectrum, 2009](https://spectrum.ieee.org/amp/blue-brain-project-leader-angry-about-cat-brain-2650252456). Hala Point's 1.15 × 10^9 neurons should be read the same way: neuromorphic systems run hand-designed or trained networks and lack the memory per neuron for measured biophysics, and Intel's archiving of Lava shows how volatile the platforms are, which NIR [Pedersen et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52259-9) tries to answer. The cat count also needs stating. IBM's abstract claims only to exceed the scale of the cat cortex; the cortical figure of about 7.6 × 10^8 that this notebook's README and chapters 06 and 08 trace to it was not confirmed by search, nor tied to Roth and Dicke 2005. The only direct count is 2.5 × 10^8 cortical neurons [Jardim-Messeder et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2017.00118), a third of that; this chapter scales from the IBM-era figure when comparing with the 2009 run, and the direct count lowers every cat estimate here threefold.

**Reconstruction versus function.** Critics of Blue Brain and the Human Brain Project argue that on the order of a billion euros bought no model that learns or behaves; defenders point to tools (NEURON and CoreNEURON, SONATA, EBRAINS), atlases and the microcircuit itself [Markram et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.09.029). Spaun performs tasks with far fewer neurons [Eliasmith et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1225266) but copies no particular brain, which is what this project cares about. The value-for-money question is unresolved.

**Does structure plus simple dynamics suffice?** The fly model's validated predictions support "structure carries function" for insect sensorimotor circuits [Shiu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07763-9). Sceptics note that it used synapse counts as weights, ignored neuromodulators, gap junctions, glia and plasticity, and succeeded on feedforward-dominated pathways; the zebrafish glia result [Mu et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.05.050) shows what it leaves out. Transfer to recurrent mammalian cortex is open.

**What counts as reading a brain?** Recording advocates count units; emulation sceptics reply that activity without synaptic weights, molecular state and plasticity rules cannot parameterize a model, and that connectomics gives structure but is destructive and static. BCI milestones come mainly from company communications, peer-reviewed reports lag, and channel counts of about 10^3 are routinely conflated with neurons read.

**Thirty orders of magnitude.** Mechanistic levels (10^15 to 10^43 FLOPS for a human) and functional estimates centred on about 10^15 FLOP/s are routinely compared as if they measured the same thing [Sandberg and Bostrom, 2008](https://www.futureofhumanityinstitute.org/s/2008-3.pdf); [Carlsmith, 2020](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/research/how-much-computational-power-does-it-take-to-match-the-human-brain/). Copying a particular brain needs the first; matching brain-level capability needs the second.

## Open questions

1. What is the minimal fidelity at which an emulation preserves behaviour, memory and, if anything, experience: point neurons, compartments, synaptic molecular state, or lower? No experiment yet discriminates these for any mammal.
2. Can synaptic weights, short-term dynamics and plasticity rules be measured at scale, or only inferred from activity, and with what identifiability? Do glia, neuromodulators and gap junctions raise the required level further?
3. Can the fly result (about 10^5 neurons) be reproduced for a mouse (about 7 × 10^7) once a whole-brain mouse connectome exists, and what fails first, dynamics or data?
4. Is there any non-destructive route to a mammalian connectome, or is emulation necessarily a destructive scan followed by reconstruction?
5. Will electrode recording keep its 6.3-year doubling, saturate on tissue damage, heat and bandwidth, or be superseded by optical and molecular readout, and what is the ceiling for chronic human implants?
6. How should an emulation be validated (population statistics, single-trial behaviour, or the specific animal's memories), and what does compartmental fidelity (10^20 to 10^22 FLOPS) really cost once memory and communication limits are counted?

## Where to start

1. **Run the fly brain in a browser (free, one afternoon).** The connectome-constrained Drosophila LIF model (Brian2; FlyWire v630, also v783) has a Colab notebook for activating or silencing neurons by FlyWire ID and watching the predicted spiking: https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model. Read the code to see which assumptions it makes.
2. **Read the two free compute reports.** Carlsmith (2020) for functional estimates, then the Sandberg and Bostrom roadmap for the fidelity table. Eliasmith's How to Build a Brain [Eliasmith, 2013](https://compneuro.uwaterloo.ca/publications/eliasmith2013.html) (Oxford University Press; paid) is the functional-modelling counterpoint.
3. **Watch the UCL Neuropixels Course (free).** Run since 2017; playlists for 2020 to 2025, manuals and probe papers are linked at https://github.com/Julie-Fabre/awesome_neuropixels.
4. **Install two simulators (free).** `pip install nengo` (4.1.0, May 2025; the framework behind Spaun; https://www.nengo.ai/nengo) and `pip install nest-simulator` (3.10.0, June 2026; the standard large-scale point-neuron simulator; https://nest-simulator.org). Build a 10^4-neuron balanced network, time it, and work out how far you are from 10^9.
5. **Run a mammalian model and look at real data (free, workstation).** BMTK tutorials (https://alleninstitute.github.io/bmtk/; `pip install bmtk`, 1.2.0, June 2026, with a GPU backend for GLIF networks) and the mouse V1 models at https://portal.brain-map.org/explore/models/mv1-all-layers; the GLIF version runs on a workstation, the biophysical version needs a cluster. Then the IBL brain-wide map through the ONE API (https://github.com/int-brain-lab/paper-brain-wide-map) and the FlyWire connectome through fafbseg-py (`pip install fafbseg`, 3.2.2, February 2026) and Codex (https://codex.flywire.ai).
